# Directing pedagogy research

A reference compiled to ground the 12-week curriculum's redesign in how
real, renowned programs and named practitioners actually teach directing
technique — not tool mechanics, not screenwriting theory in the abstract,
but the craft: working with actors, staging, shot construction as a
directorial judgement, giving actionable notes, rehearsal technique, script
analysis for directors, and critique pedagogy.

Compiled via two research passes: one across seven academic film-directing
programs, one across five named directing methodologies/instructional
courses. Every claim below is either sourced (URL given) or explicitly
flagged as unverified/anecdotal — nothing here is presented as fact without
a citation backing it. This document records findings only; it makes no
curriculum recommendations itself (see `PLAN.md` for the revision proposal
built from it).

## Academic film school directing programs

### AFI Conservatory
No fixed weekly lecture/workshop split is documented — courses blend
"lectures, case studies and workshops" within one course. **Directing
Actors I & II** (required, both terms) has fellows use their own body/voice
as actors, to learn how actors rehearse and prep scenes — a director
learning the craft by first standing in the actor's position. **Rehearsal
Process Lab** (2nd year) targets movement, actors' intentions, scene
transitions, and abstracts character relationships into exercises. Feedback
runs on **mentor critiques** (two per cycle film — one on script/story, one
on the "Director's Book"), a formal **Comprehensive Review** interview
presenting workbooks + self-analysis, and a documented "dailies review"
model (faculty + peers reviewing dailies/edits in a classroom setting).
Sources: [conservatory.afi.com/directing-curriculum](https://conservatory.afi.com/directing-curriculum/), [conservatory.afi.com/directing-second-year](https://conservatory.afi.com/directing-second-year/), [conservatory.afi.com/directing](https://conservatory.afi.com/directing/)

### NYU Tisch (Graduate Film)
No official lecture/workshop split stated. Directing/cinematography
coursework explicitly treats blocking, movement, composition, POV and
visual flow as *directorial* choices, not just cinematography. Best-sourced
detail: during editing, the whole class convenes in a **weekly Master
Class with all directing teachers** to screen every student's cuts for
group critique, plus a mid-semester full-faculty critique. Unverified: no
source found for scene-study or rehearsal exercises specific to NYU's
directing courses (only the editing-critique format is documented).
Sources: [tisch.nyu.edu/grad-film/courses](https://tisch.nyu.edu/grad-film/courses), [tisch.nyu.edu/grad-film/faculty/directing-faculty](https://tisch.nyu.edu/grad-film/faculty/directing-faculty)

### USC School of Cinematic Arts
Least documented of the seven. Official pages describe the MFA only in
general terms; cross-training (writers taking directing courses, working
directly with actors) is a named policy. No verifiable source for a
specific rehearsal/blocking/critique format at the graduate directing
level — forum accounts exist but are explicitly not treated as fact here.
Sources: [catalogue.usc.edu MFA program page](https://catalogue.usc.edu/preview_program.php?catoid=12&poid=12576), [cinema.usc.edu/production](http://cinema.usc.edu/production/index.cfm)

### UCLA School of Theater, Film and Television
Directing MFA runs in three phases — Year 1 "Art of Collaboration"
(culminates in a 2–5 min sync-sound short), Year 2 "Faculty Mentorship"
(one-on-one advanced project), Years 3–4 thesis production. An
undergraduate lecture course (FTV 122M, 3 hrs) is the clearest documented
case of a discrete "directing lecture" format, explicitly naming directing
actors, blocking/camera coverage, and on-set practice as its topics. A
"dailies reviewed and critiqued in class" format is documented for UCLA
**Extension's** (non-MFA) Advanced Filmmaking Workshop, not confirmed for
the MFA itself.
Sources: [tft.ucla.edu/programs/production-directing-mfa](https://www.tft.ucla.edu/programs/production-directing-mfa/), [legacy.tft.ucla.edu/mfadirectingscholarship-curriculum](https://legacy.tft.ucla.edu/mfadirectingscholarship-curriculum)

### Columbia University School of the Arts
Best-documented shared-core structure: all first-year MFA students take
**Directing I & II**, **Directing Actors I & II**, **Fundamentals of
Directing**, **Practical Production I & II**, in studios capped at 12. A
first-hand (unofficial, forum) alumnus account draws a sharp, useful
distinction: **"Directing Actors"** is live, proscenium-style rehearsal
work with professional actors in the room, evaluated on the actor
direction itself; **standard directing exercises** evaluate shot
selection, editing, blocking and pacing — not acting quality. A scene
rehearsed in one class is sometimes later filmed for the other.
Sources: [arts.columbia.edu/film/graduate/screenwriting-directing/mfa-curriculum](https://arts.columbia.edu/film/graduate/screenwriting-directing/mfa-curriculum), [arts.columbia.edu/film/faq](https://arts.columbia.edu/film/faq) (alumnus detail via filmschool.org, flagged anecdotal)

### London Film School
Continuous, non-modular practical training; workshops "with actors,
directors and editors" are explicit. Best-documented concrete exercise:
quarterly **Actors Table Readings** with an external actor-supply partner,
and ahead of later-term shorts, a dedicated **3-hour directing-practice
session** where directors choose short scenes and spend the session
staging/redirecting passages with actors specifically to build
director–actor communication and casting instinct. No official source for
LFS's critique/screening structure.
Sources: [nothingtoperform.com/london-film-schools](https://nothingtoperform.com/london-film-schools), [lfs.org.uk/full-time-study/ma-screenwriting/curriculum](https://lfs.org.uk/full-time-study/ma-screenwriting/curriculum)

### National Film and Television School (NFTS)
**Directing Fiction** (2-year MA) is preceded by a 4-week Foundation Course
for incoming fiction directors, then "practical experience of filmmaking,
combined with a wide-ranging series of workshops emphasising performance,
mise-en-scène and an examination of how narrative works." Facilities
include 2 cinemas used specifically for **rushes** (dailies) and
screenings, implying a rushes-review critique culture — no page details
its exact structure. No official source names a specific blocking or
rehearsal exercise.
Sources: [nfts.co.uk/directing-fiction](https://nfts.co.uk/directing-fiction), [prospects.ac.uk directing-fiction](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/universities/national-film-and-television-school-20379/courses/directing-fiction-126545)

**Cross-program pattern**: no official page gives a minute-by-minute
lecture/workshop split. The recurring, well-sourced structural element
across programs is a **regular screening-and-critique ritual** (AFI's
dailies review, NYU's weekly Master Class, NFTS's rushes cinemas) — work in
progress is screened to a group on a fixed cadence, not just marked at the
end. The second recurring element is **directors training as actors first**
(AFI's Directing Actors I/II, Columbia's Directing Actors track) before or
alongside directing others.

## Named directing methodologies

### Judith Weston — *Directing Actors*
Core technique, **"the moment before"**: invent the concrete, playable
offstage life immediately preceding a scene, rather than relying on script
parentheticals ("pause," "beat") — Weston calls those unplayable writer's
subtext. Exercises: improvising the off-camera scene just before the
written scene starts; "half-and-half" improv blending scripted and
improvised lines; three-part paraphrase of dialogue to internalize it
without stiff delivery. Her own course (from 1988, "Acting for Directors")
runs as an intensive 3-day workshop mixing acting exercises with technique
demonstration, plus a longer 5-day seminar doing deep script subtext
analysis (subworld, "as ifs," spines, imagery, themes). A recorded 3-hour
Master Class (Sundance Collab) covers actor rapport, scene breakdown, and
casting prep.
Sources: [Ch. 9 rehearsal notes](https://www.studocu.com/en-us/document/georgia-state-university/actingdirecting-for-the-camera/ch-9-rehearsal-notes-from-directing-actors-by-judith-weston/47736506), [judithweston.com](https://judithweston.com/web/), [Sundance Collab](https://collab.sundance.org/catalog/Master-Class-Directing-Actors-with-Judith-Weston-Video)

### David Mamet — *On Directing Film*
Core technique, **"shot as argument"**: build meaning through juxtaposition
of uninflected shots (cut face → cut fridge interior = "hunger") rather
than through performance, narration or camera movement — explicitly derived
from Stanislavski's objective/super-objective framework and Eisenstein
montage theory. Prescribed exercise (the book itself, from his Columbia
lectures): build a shot list line-by-line from a script, filtered strictly
through "what does the character want, will they get it." Rejects
coverage-heavy/Steadicam shooting in favor of deliberate cuts.
Sources: [Musicbed](https://www.musicbed.com/articles/filmmaking/directing/how-to-make-a-scene-david-mamets-advice-to-directors-book-giveaway/), [Alec Nevala-Lee](https://nevalalee.wordpress.com/2015/11/12/my-great-books-9-on-directing-film/), [eNotes](https://www.enotes.com/topics/david-mamet/criticism/mamet-david-vol-166/gay-brewer-review-date-1992)

### Sanford Meisner (as applied to directing)
Core principle: "living truthfully under imaginary circumstances" —
truthful real-time response to a scene partner over premeditated delivery.
Signature exercise: the **repetition exercise** (two actors repeating a
neutral phrase while reacting to each other's real behavior). No
Meisner-authored or Institute-published curriculum specifically for
directors was found — Sydney Pollack's Meisner training (Neighborhood
Playhouse, 1952–54) is the documented link between the technique and
directing, not a Meisner-for-directors course itself. Treat "Meisner for
directors" as inference, not documented pedagogy.
Sources: [StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/meisner-technique/), [ActorLab](https://actorlab.io/blog/meisner-technique-guide)

### MasterClass instructors
- **Scorsese** ("Teaches Filmmaking," 30 lessons/~5h, 76-page workbook):
  script development, working with crew by asking questions not dictating,
  editing philosophy; homework includes watching assigned films and making
  a short. No named technique-vocabulary of its own.
- **Ron Howard** ("Teaches Directing," 32 lessons/~7h48m): on-set footage
  of real staging/blocking decisions explained live; workbook has a
  cinematography case-study assignment on a favorite film.
- **Herzog** ("Teaches Filmmaking," ~6h): concrete exercise — build a
  pinhole camera, shoot one landscape + one portrait, before "earning" a
  real camera; deconstructs *Viva Zapata!*'s opening scene for analysis.
- **Spike Lee** ("Teaches Independent Filmmaking"): dedicated lesson
  "Collaborating and Improvising with Actors"; a lesson on adapting to
  script changes via improv (*He Got Game*); casting flagged by reviewers
  as the most in-depth lesson.
- **David Lynch** ("Teaches Creativity and Film," 13 lessons/~2h52m):
  deliberately non-technical — catching ideas, "thinking cinematically" —
  no procedural directing technique.
Sources: [Scorsese](https://www.masterclass.com/classes/martin-scorsese-teaches-filmmaking), [Howard](https://www.masterclass.com/classes/ron-howard-teaches-directing) (via [Jonny Elwyn review](https://jonnyelwyn.co.uk/film-and-video-editing/ron-howard-directing-masterclass-reviewed/)), [Herzog](https://www.masterclass.com/classes/werner-herzog-teaches-filmmaking), [Lee](https://www.masterclass.com/classes/spike-lee-teaches-filmmaking), [Lynch](https://www.masterclass.com/classes/david-lynch-teaches-creativity-and-film)

### Sydney Pollack
Confirmed: trained under Meisner at the Neighborhood Playhouse (1952–54),
later taught acting there before directing — the documented basis for his
reputation directing actors (12 Oscar-nominated performances under him).
No specific published/transcribed lecture or master class on his directing
technique could be verified — only tribute material and one documentary
(*The Films of Sydney Pollack*) turned up, with no indexed transcript.
Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Pollack)

## Cross-cutting patterns worth building on

Read across both passes, not from any single source:

1. **A regular screen-and-critique ritual**, not just end-of-project
   marking — AFI's dailies review, NYU's weekly Master Class, NFTS's
   rushes cinemas, Columbia's studio critiques.
2. **Directors trained as actors first**, or at minimum put through the
   actor's side of a rehearsal (AFI Directing Actors I/II, Columbia
   Directing Actors track) — before or alongside directing others.
3. **Two distinct workshop modes**, not one generic "workshop": a
   rehearsal-based, actor-in-the-room mode judged on direction quality
   (Columbia's "Directing Actors"), and a construction-based mode judged
   on shot/edit/pacing decisions (Columbia's "standard directing
   exercises," Mamet's shot-list method).
4. **An objective/tactic framing for notes** (Mamet, via Stanislavski):
   a note is grounded in what the character wants and whether they get it,
   not a mood word.
5. **A concrete antecedent-circumstance technique** (Weston's "moment
   before") for turning a vague reaction into something playable/directable.
