## BIS-W03-SCENE

Canonical text, clean: [scene.txt](./scene.txt). Canonical text,
annotated: [scene-annotated.txt](./scene-annotated.txt).

One scene, standard screenplay format, well under two pages, in her late
mother's apartment kitchen: Theo calls, Nadia's hand drifts toward the
phone and stops, and on the last ring before the call would go to
voicemail she answers and says "Hey," then listens. `scene.txt` is the
clean reading copy, with no notes present at all, for the cold read the
lecture asks students to do first. `scene-annotated.txt` is the identical
scene with two director's intent notes reinserted in the margin, each
naming a concrete antecedent circumstance rather than prescribing a mood,
speed, or confidence level: one on the held, unmoving look while the phone
rings (she has just taped the last box from her mother's kitchen shut,
seconds before the phone lit up with Theo's name), and one on the line
"Hey" (her hand has already pressed to accept the call before she's
registered deciding to; Theo can hear her now).

## Scope and teaching checks

- Follows directly from week 1's demonstration beat (reach, stop short,
  withdraw) and week 2's logline/premise: this is the scene in which the
  premise's ending - Nadia accepting the call on the last ring, saying
  "Hey," then listening - actually plays out on the page, including the
  reach-and-stop beat on the way there.
- Splitting the scene into a clean copy and an annotated copy keeps the
  lecture's cold-read exercise genuinely blind: the file linked before the
  reveal (`scene.txt`) carries no notes at all, so a student's prediction
  can't be short-circuited by reading ahead. `scene-annotated.txt` exists
  solely for after that reveal, to show both notes attached to the exact
  lines they govern.
- Both notes name a concrete antecedent circumstance - something that has
  just happened, playable and observable - not an adjective or a
  prescribed result (a mood word, a speed, a degree of confidence). This
  is the distinction week 3's lecture teaches (Weston's "moment before").
  Corrected 2026-09-12: the previous version of both notes ("active
  restraint," "too fast," "not casual," "not confident," "a decision that
  outran itself") named the desired result rather than what happened
  before it, which contradicted the lecture's own teaching point. What a
  performer's face or voice might actually do in response to either
  circumstance is a directing judgement made from the note, not something
  the note states outright - the lecture discusses that judgement
  separately from the note itself, and does not fold it back into the
  note text.
- Does not resolve whether the call connects or what Theo says - the scene
  ends on Nadia answering, consistent with week 2's premise not claiming
  more than an opening of contact.
- Students bring their own scene and note, as session 03 requires; this
  text is the lecture's worked example only.

## BIS-W03-WORKSHEET

Canonical text: [scene-planning-worksheet.txt](./scene-planning-worksheet.txt).

A downloadable, fill-in template for turning a premise into a two-page
scene, added 2026-09-12. Walks the same sequence the lecture now teaches
(premise → decisive scene → scene change → five observable beats →
screenplay form → director's note) as blank sections for a student's own
film — not a second copy of the "Before It Stops" example. Sections:
story anchor (protagonist/want/obstacle/immediate consequence), scene
change (beginning/end/visible change), five observable beats, screenplay
conversion, director's intent note (with the "You have just ___. Now
___." frame from the lecture), and a final checklist that maps directly
onto session 03's three `spec:` bullets.

This is scaffolding, not a graded artefact and not owner evidence: no
individual worksheet field is separately assessed, and it does not
satisfy `OWN-W03` (the owner's own scene + intent note). It is a teaching
aid for reaching the same three outcomes `03-screenplay-workshop.md`
already checks.

Served copy: `public/resources/week-03/scene-planning-worksheet.txt`.
Linked from the Week 3 lecture (writing-process section) and the Week 3
workshop's "Before the workshop" section, with the same instruction in
both places: download and read it beforehand, then fill it in across the
workshop's activities, using your own Week 2 premise. Update canonical and
served copies together.
