Screenplay form exists so a director can say what happens without saying how
it feels — that's what the note in the margin is for. This week is about
learning the form well enough to use it as a tool, not a template.

## Outline

- screenplay form as a director's tool: sluglines, action, dialogue, and why
  the blank space on the page matters as much as the words on it
- action lines write what a camera can see; dialogue is what a character is
  willing to say out loud, which is usually not what they mean
- a director's intent note in the margin — *"she says this too fast, before
  she's decided to"* — points at a performance choice, not a plot beat
- "Table for One": drafting the closing scene — Del sliding the plate across
  the counter, Mara deciding whether to eat it
