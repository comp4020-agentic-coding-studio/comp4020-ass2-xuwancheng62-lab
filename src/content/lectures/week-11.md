A film with picture locked and no sound pass is still a rough cut, no matter
how finished the images look. Sound is where the last third of the audience's
attention actually lives.

## Outline

- directing a voice performance the same way you'd direct a face: a note
  aimed at pace, breath, or hesitation
- ambience and Foley as a location's second bible — what a room sounds like
  is as fixed as what it looks like
- music as a directorial statement about how a moment should feel, not
  wallpaper laid under a scene
- scoring the last line of "Table for One" twice, with and without a music
  cue, and hearing exactly what the cue is doing
