An anchor frame is a locked starting point, generated straight from the
bible so it matches everything already decided. What happens next is where
directing actually starts: not what's in the frame, but what changes inside
it over the next few seconds.

## Outline

- the anchor frame as a locked starting point, not a fresh guess — generated
  from the bible, not from a new description
- prompting image-to-video for performance: what changes in a face, a hand, a
  held breath — not what's in the room
- timing and camera movement as directable variables, exactly like a line
  reading
- animating the plate-slide from "Table for One": the anchor frame, then two
  directed attempts at the timing of Del letting go of the plate
