## Before the workshop

Bring your own Week 2 premise — the want, the obstacle, and what happens
if the want fails. Download and read the
[scene-planning worksheet](/resources/week-03/scene-planning-worksheet.txt)
so you arrive knowing the sequence it walks you through. You'll fill it in
during the workshop itself, not before it.

## In the workshop

Total workshop time: 120 minutes, across preparation, five writing
activities, a peer-review activity, and a completion check.

### Activity 1 — Recap and task briefing (10 min)

**Instructions.** A quick recap of the lecture's writing-process
sequence — premise → decisive scene → scene change → five observable
beats → screenplay form → director's note — and its antecedent-circumstance
move: a note names what just happened a moment before, not an adjective
describing how the line should land. Today you run that sequence on your
own premise using the worksheet, then test your finished note on a
partner using only the isolated line or action it belongs to. Nothing on
the worksheet is graded by itself; you'll be assessed on the three `spec`
bullets by the end of the session.

**Expected output.** A clear sense of the six-step sequence and what the
session will check at the end.

### Activity 2 — Story anchor (10 min)

**Instructions.** Fill in worksheet section A from your own premise: who
wants what, the concrete obstacle in this scene, and what happens
immediately if they fail.

**Expected output.** Worksheet section A complete, naming your want,
obstacle, and immediate consequence.

### Activity 3 — Scene change (10 min)

**Instructions.** Fill in worksheet section B before writing a single
line of screenplay form: the scene's beginning state, its end state, and
the visible change between them. If you can't name a beginning and an end
that differ, pick a different moment from your premise.

**Expected output.** Worksheet section B complete, with a beginning and
end state that visibly differ.

### Activity 4 — Five observable beats (15 min)

**Instructions.** Fill in worksheet section C: five sentences, each
something an audience can see or hear — not a shot, a camera move, or a
cut. Those decisions belong to week 4's shot breakdown, not to the scene
you're writing today.

**Expected output.** Five observable beats, in order, covering the
distance from your scene's beginning state to its end state.

### Activity 5 — Screenplay conversion (25 min)

**Instructions.** Convert your five beats directly into standard
screenplay form: slugline, action, dialogue — no feeling stated directly
in either column, and dialogue only where a beat actually needs a line
spoken out loud.

**Expected output.** One scene in standard screenplay form, no longer
than two pages — the artefact the first `spec` bullet checks.

### Activity 6 — Director's note (10 min)

**Instructions.** Choose the one line or action from your finished scene
where the visible change actually happens, and write an intent note for
it using the antecedent-circumstance move: what concrete event has just
happened, and what fact is the character responding to right now? Check
your own note against the lecture's distinction before moving on: does it
name something that just happened, or does it secretly reduce to an
adjective ("nervously," "furious," "sad") in different words?

**Expected output.** At least one written director's intent note, naming
a concrete antecedent circumstance — the artefact the second `spec`
bullet checks.

### Activity 7 — Dailies review (30 min)

**Instructions.** Trade roles once, presenting your own note and
reviewing your partner's. Hand your partner only the isolated line or
action and its intent note, copied out or shown on its own — not the rest
of the scene, and not your intended interpretation.

- **As the reviewer**, first read the bare line or action on its own and
  form an initial impression of how it might be played. Then read the
  note attached to it. Describe one or more concrete performance choices
  the circumstance could motivate, and say plainly how the note changed
  your reading from what you had before you saw it.
- **As the director**, listen without correcting. Only once your partner
  has described their reading do you reveal the surrounding scene and the
  dramatic function you intended for the moment, and compare: does their
  reading line up with what you intended? If it doesn't, is that because
  the note was actually an adjective in disguise, or because it named a
  genuine antecedent circumstance that still left room for more than one
  reading?

Swap roles partway through and repeat, so both partners have reviewed and
been reviewed.

**Expected output.** A partner's independent description of one or more
performance choices motivated by your isolated line/action and its note,
plus their account of how the note changed their reading — the artefact
the third `spec` bullet checks.

### Activity 8 — Completion check (10 min)

**Instructions.** Walk the three `spec` bullets as a checklist, using
worksheet section F as your guide: a scene in proper form, no longer than
two pages; at least one intent note naming a concrete antecedent
circumstance; and your partner's independent reading of the isolated line
or action plus its note, compared against your intended dramatic
function.

**Expected output.** All three `spec` bullets satisfied and submitted
together — the worksheet itself stays with you as working notes, not a
submission.

## Afterwards

The scene and its intent note travel forward together: next week you break
this exact scene into shots.
