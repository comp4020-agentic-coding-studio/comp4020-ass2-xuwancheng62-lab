## Before the workshop

Bring a logline for your own short — not "Before It Stops", your own — and a
one-paragraph premise underneath it.

## In the workshop

### Activity 1 — Recap and task briefing (10 min)

**Instructions:** Recap the lecture's objective/obstacle test in one
sentence before starting: a logline is a claim with two testable parts,
not a summary. Today's task: bring your own logline and premise, test them
on a partner, and revise if the test fails. You'll be assessed on all
three `spec` bullets by the end of the session.

**Expected output:** Your own logline and premise in front of you, and a
clear statement of today's task and how it will be checked.

### Activity 2 — Warm-up: test a familiar film (10 min)

**Instructions:** Ungraded — this doesn't touch today's `spec` bullets.
Pick one film you already know well from this list: *Jaws, Finding Nemo,
Die Hard, Toy Story, The Matrix, Alien, Mad Max: Fury Road, WALL-E.* In
about two minutes, write a one-sentence logline for it naming the
protagonist's objective and obstacle. Trade with a partner: they read only
your sentence and state the obstacle back, the same way you'll verify each
other's real loglines later. This is the dailies-review mechanic
rehearsed on a story that's already finished, before you do it on one
that isn't.

**Expected output:** A tested one-sentence logline for a familiar film,
and a first run at the dailies-review mechanic — your partner's obstacle
stated back correctly, or not.

### Activity 3 — Write and pair up (70 min)

**Instructions:** Working from the logline and premise you brought,
refine your logline against the objective/obstacle test from the lecture.
Check the premise with the lecture's scope test too: name the essential
observable actions, the visible change, and their estimated screen time.
Can they fit two to four minutes, one location, and one or two characters
without padding or exceeding those limits? Revise the premise if they
cannot. These estimates help you explain filmability; they are not
measured production durations.

Then check it against this week's good-story criterion too: does your
premise open on an emotional hook — something the audience already feels
— rather than a recap of who everyone is? Are you relying on dialogue
exposition or internal monologue to carry feeling the audience should be
reading in behaviour instead? If either has crept in, cut it and replace
it with an observable action, the way "Before It Stops" replaces backstory
with the phone ringing.

Then pair up. Read your logline aloud to your partner — premise still
face-down or unread — and ask the class's one standing question: *so what
happens if they don't get it?* If you can't answer it in a sentence, the
logline isn't finished yet; revise it on the spot and re-test before
moving on.

**Expected output:** A logline that passes the objective/obstacle test and
survives the "so what happens if they don't get it?" question, and a
premise that passes the scope test and the emotional-hook criterion.

### Activity 4 — Dailies review (20 min)

**Instructions:** Trade roles twice, once presenting your own logline,
once reviewing your partner's:

- **As the reviewer**, having read only your partner's logline — not their
  premise — state their protagonist's obstacle back in your own words. You
  are not repeating their sentence; you are saying what you understood the
  obstacle to be.
- **As the director**, listen without correcting. Only once your partner
  has stated the obstacle do you reveal your premise and say whether they
  got it — and if they didn't, which part of the logline let them down: no
  stated obstacle, an obstacle that read as a mood rather than something
  concrete, or stakes that overshadowed it.

Swap roles and repeat, then compare: did stating the obstacle back
correctly take one read of the logline, or did it need the premise to make
sense? A logline that only works with the premise attached hasn't passed
the test yet.

**Expected output:** Both partners' obstacles stated back from the
logline alone, each confirmed or corrected against the premise, and a
verdict on whether either logline still needs the premise attached to
make sense.

### Activity 5 — Completion check (10 min)

**Instructions:** Walk the three `spec` bullets as a checklist: a logline
naming protagonist, want, and obstacle; a premise that's filmable in two
to four minutes with one or two characters and one location; and your
partner's independent read of the obstacle from the logline alone. Submit
all three together.

**Expected output:** All three `spec` bullets confirmed against your
logline and premise, submitted together.

## Afterwards

Your logline is the fixed point every later week's work gets checked against.
If it changes later, that's expected — but it changes on purpose, not by
drift.
