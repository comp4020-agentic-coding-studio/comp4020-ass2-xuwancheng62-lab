## Before the workshop

Bring a bible entry for every named character and location in your short.
Read the Week 5 lecture first. You can use the
[printable bible worksheet](/resources/week-05/bible-worksheet.html) to prepare
those drafts; repeat the character and location pages as needed. The
[worked teaching entries](/resources/week-05/bible-examples.txt) show the
method, including explicitly unresolved fields. They do not replace your
own entries. Images are optional; this workshop requires no generation.

## In the workshop

### Activity 1 — Recap and inventory (10 min)

**Instructions:** State the lecture's distinction between fixed identity
and changing action state. List every named character and location in your
short and match each to an entry. Read both spec bullets above before
starting. Name missing entries now; use Activity 2 to fill them.

**Expected output:** An inventory of characters and locations, matched to
drafts, with any missing entries identified.

### Activity 2 — Make details repeatable (25 min)

**Instructions:** Work individually through your drafts. Replace vague
adjectives with observable details. Include appearance, wardrobe and voice
where relevant, and spatial relationships and light for locations. Identify
fixed facts separately from shot-specific changes. Resolve needed design
choices; if you cannot, mark the field pending and explain what decision is
needed. Record the reference/version when using one. Prioritise missing
entries, then your protagonist, before refining the rest.

**Expected output:** Concrete character and location entries, with remaining
unresolved fields visible rather than disguised by adjectives.

### Activity 3 — Blind partner read-back (20 min)

**Instructions:** Pair with someone who has not seen your project. Exchange
only the protagonist entries, including any reference explicitly attached
to the entry. Do not show the screenplay or give an oral introduction. Each
reader writes and then describes the protagonist back: their appearance,
costume, distinguishing detail and voice where specified. Mark unspecified
facts as unknown rather than guessing. Save both accounts before either
author explains anything. Split the time evenly between projects.

**Expected output:** A written independent account of each protagonist,
recorded before author explanation. If no unfamiliar classmate is available,
record that the blind criterion still needs a later check.

### Activity 4 — Diagnose and revise (30 min)

**Instructions:** Compare each account to the entry, not to an unspoken
intention. For each mismatch, point to the relevant words and distinguish
contradiction, reader omission and ambiguous or missing detail. Reread a
missed fact; revise text where it leaves a necessary choice open. Do not
accept an oral explanation as a repair to the entry. Apply the same audit
to your other character and location entries. Record changes and any
reference decisions still outstanding.

**Expected output:** Revised entries with specific reasons for changes,
and a record of any unresolved design choices.

### Activity 5 — Dailies review and recheck (25 min)

**Instructions:** Share revised entries in pairs or a small group. Where
possible, exchange with a new reader for another protagonist read-back.
Otherwise use your returning partner and label the retest as informed by
the first pass. Check a location entry too: can the reader describe where
the important objects and character belong? Discuss one fixed fact and one
allowed action change. This location check supports the first spec bullet;
it is not an additional graded criterion.

**Expected output:** A second account checked against the revised entry,
reader familiarity noted, and feedback on the location's spatial clarity.

### Activity 6 — Completion check (10 min)

**Instructions:** Walk the two spec bullets together. Is there a concrete
entry for every named character and location? Has an unfamiliar classmate
correctly described the protagonist from the entry alone? Check needed
pending fields and any contradicted facts before calling it complete. If
only an informed retest was possible after a failed blind read, name the
fresh-reader check still needed. Template completion alone is not a pass.

**Expected output:** Both original spec bullets verified, or a named gap
with the decision or peer check needed to finish it.

## Afterwards

Keep the revised entries with your pre-production package for Week 6.
Use the current agreed reference for later shots; record deliberate changes
and identify which shots need updating. A consistent reference defines what
to check, not a guarantee that a generated result will match it.
