## Before the workshop

Nothing to install or sign up for. Your instructor provides the clip.
You will observe it, write a redirect, and review a partner's note. You
will not generate another video in this session.

## In the workshop

### Activity 1 — Recap and locked scene (10 min)

**Instructions.** Recap the lecture's chain: intention, objective,
playable action, result, interpretation. Today's scene is a deliberate
counterfactual: what if Nadia had not answered her brother's call? The
ringing has stopped and the call has gone to voicemail. She sits at the
kitchen table at night, holding the phone. This is a rehearsal variation,
not the story's canonical continuation.

Keep Nadia, her wardrobe, the kitchen, phone, lighting and static camera
fixed when writing your redirect. The objective and intended interpretation
will be revealed after your first viewing.

**Expected output.** A shared understanding of the scene and what must
stay fixed.

### Activity 2 — Watch Take A cold (15 min)

**Instructions.** Watch the provided clip before seeing the objective
or any production note. Write what Nadia does, roughly when, and what
you think she is trying to accomplish. Separate observable actions from
your interpretation of them.

**Expected output.** Three or more timed observations and your initial
reading of Nadia's intention.

### Activity 3 — Reveal and diagnose (20 min)

**Instructions.** Read the locked information:

- **Objective:** Nadia wants to call her brother back, but only once
  she is sure she can do it without her voice breaking when he answers.
- **Intended interpretation:** her pause should read as steadying
  herself to reconnect, rather than regret, avoidance, or having changed
  her mind about calling.

Watch again. Choose one observable problem that interferes with this
reading. Name its approximate time, what you actually see, and how it
changes your interpretation. You may consider timing, gaze, gesture,
breath, emphasis, degree, or unwanted speaking. There is no required
single diagnosis.

This is an imperfect generated clip. Distinguish a performance choice
that could be redirected from a generation artefact, such as a distorted
hand. A generation artefact is worth recording, but choose a performance
problem for your Note B. Do not invent an action that the clip does not
show or change the objective to make the clip fit.

**Expected output.** A diagnosis naming one observable performance
problem, its location, and its effect on your reading.

### Activity 4 — Write Note B (25 min)

**Instructions.** Write a short playable redirect that addresses your
chosen problem. Tell the performer what to do, when, or in relation to
what, rather than asking for a result such as “be more emotional.” Keep
the scene, character, objective, intended interpretation and camera fixed.

Under your note, explain how the proposed action responds to your diagnosis.
Describe the observable change you would look for in a future take. This
is a prediction; no new take is being generated or selected today.

**Expected output.** A first Note B, its rationale, and an observable
criterion for checking a future result.

### Activity 5 — Partner review and revision (35 min)

**Instructions.** Exchange your diagnosis and note with a partner.
Each reviewer replays the relevant moment in Take A and checks:

- Does the diagnosis describe something actually visible in the clip?
- Does the note target that problem and give a playable action?
- Does it preserve the locked objective and scene?
- Does the proposed observation make it possible to check a future take?

Spend 10 minutes reviewing each person's work. Give specific written
feedback, then use 10 minutes to revise your own note. In the final five
minutes, a few pairs share one diagnosis and the corresponding revision.

Peer review checks the written redirect's reasoning and specificity.
It cannot establish whether the redirect would improve an actual new
performance; that requires generating or performing another take later.

**Expected output.** A partner's written check and a revised Note B,
with a brief explanation of what you changed or why you kept it.

### Activity 6 — Completion check (15 min)

**Instructions.** Submit your timed diagnosis, first Note B, partner's
feedback, revised Note B, and the observable change you would look for
in a future take. Check them against the four spec criteria above.
Clearly label predicted effects as predictions.

**Expected output.** One complete diagnosis-and-redirect record.

## Afterwards

You leave with an evidence-based diagnosis and a reviewed redirect.
The lecture demonstrated how a new take can test a note; today's exercise
practises writing a note precise enough to make that later test possible.
