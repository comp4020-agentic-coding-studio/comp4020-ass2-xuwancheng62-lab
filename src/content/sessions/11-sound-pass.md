## Before the workshop

Bring your chosen cut from week 10, with one line still undirected.

## In the workshop

This workshop runs in two phases: rehearsal, directing the voice before
anything is mixed in, then construction, building the sound pass around
the directed take.

### Activity 1 — Recap and line selection (10 min)

**Instructions:** State the lecture's rule that a voice note needs the same
concrete target as a face note — pace, breath, or hesitation, not a mood
word. Identify the one undirected line in your cut and write the single
note you'll direct it with.

**Expected output:** One chosen line and one concrete, playable note.

### Activity 2 — Direct two takes (rehearsal) (30 min)

**Instructions:** Generate two takes of the line from your single note,
varying only what the note targets. Listen to both before deciding
anything.

**Expected output:** Two directed takes of the same line.

### Activity 3 — Select and lay in (rehearsal) (10 min)

**Instructions:** Select the take that serves the beat and write one
sentence saying why, against the beat's intent — not against which take
sounded better in isolation. Lay the selected take into your cut.

**Expected output:** A cut with the directed line laid in, and a written
selection justification.

### Activity 4 — Build the sound pass (construction) (40 min)

**Instructions:** Working from your locked location's sound signature, add
one ambience bed, at least one Foley or SFX moment, and one music cue
placed on purpose — not under the whole scene by default. State what claim
the cue's placement makes before finalizing where it starts.

**Expected output:** A full sound-pass version of your cut: ambience, at
least one Foley/SFX moment, and one deliberately placed music cue.

### Activity 5 — Compare and completion check (construction) (30 min)

**Instructions:** Watch the dialogue-only and full-pass versions back to
back. Name, beat by beat, what the sound pass changed emotionally — not
just that it "feels more finished." Walk the three spec bullets: two
directed takes with a selection justification; two cut versions; a
beat-by-beat account of what changed.

**Expected output:** A beat-by-beat comparison note, and all three spec
bullets verified.

## Afterwards

Wherever the sound pass changed the meaning of a beat, note it — that note is
part of what the final director's statement asks for.
