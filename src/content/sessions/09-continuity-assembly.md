## Before the workshop

Bring the shots you've generated and directed so far for one sequence of your
film.

## In the workshop

### Activity 1 — Recap and assembly prep (10 min)

**Instructions:** State the lecture's distinction between what a sequence
has to match and what the eye forgives, and its three named continuity
failure types — character drift, spatial, temporal. Lay out your shots in
their intended order before cutting anything.

**Expected output:** An ordered list of shots, ready to assemble.

### Activity 2 — Cut the sequence (30 min)

**Instructions:** Cut your shots together in order into one sequence
running 30 to 60 seconds. Choose each transition on purpose — a cut, a
dissolve, a hold — and be able to say what it claims about time or
connection.

**Expected output:** A cut sequence of at least three shots, 30 to 60
seconds, with deliberate transitions.

### Activity 3 — Cold-watch and log (20 min)

**Instructions:** Watch your cut back once, cold, without pausing. Log
every continuity break you notice, naming which type it is — character
drift, spatial, or temporal — rather than a vague impression that
something's off.

**Expected output:** A continuity log with every finding named and typed.

### Activity 4 — Group dailies-review (30 min)

**Instructions:** In a small group, screen your cut without revealing your
log first. The group calls out any continuity break they notice
independently; compare their findings against your own log, the way AFI's
dailies review reads a cut cold before comparing notes. Do this for every
group member's cut in turn.

**Expected output:** Your continuity log confirmed or extended by the
group's independent read.

### Activity 5 — Repair and completion check (30 min)

**Instructions:** For each logged finding, decide fix or accept, and write
the reason. Choose a repair that addresses the named problem: adjust a trim
or cut point, make a local correction, or regenerate the affected shot where
needed. Week 9's phone-state example was repaired by trimming 1.3 seconds.
Change only what the logged finding requires. Then walk the two spec bullets:
do you have a cut sequence of at least three shots, 30 to 60 seconds? Does
your log name at least one problem and one fix-or-accept decision with a
reason?

**Expected output:** A repaired sequence touching only the flagged shots,
and both spec bullets verified.

## Afterwards

Repair only the problems your log flags, using editing, a local correction,
or regeneration as needed. Keep the rest of the sequence unchanged.
