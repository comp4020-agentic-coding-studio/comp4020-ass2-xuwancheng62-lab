## Before the workshop

Bring your complete shot list and storyboard — the whole short, not a scene
of it — plus your locked bible from week 5. For "Before It Stops," that
bible now includes the Nadia, kitchen, and phone references alongside the
text entries.

## In the workshop

### Activity 1 — Recap and inventory (10 min)

**Instructions:** State the lecture's chain: bible fixes identity, a
keyframe fixes one shot's specific still (or, for a continuous shot, its
start and end stills), and the generated shot adds performance, action,
timing, and camera movement on top. Lay out your current shot list and
storyboard and mark which shots are new since week 4, each shot's
keyframe(s), which bible reference each keyframe needs, and whether that
reference is resolved or still pending.

**Expected output:** An inventory of shots, each marked with its
keyframe(s), the bible reference(s) those keyframes need, and its
dependency status.

### Activity 2 — Expand the sequence (50 min)

**Instructions:** Work individually. Extend your storyboard so it covers
the whole short, first frame to last, not only its most dramatic scene.
For each shot, name its keyframe(s) — one for most shots, a start/end pair
for a shot built as one continuous take without a cut — and the bible
reference(s) each keyframe draws on. Then build a **preparation list**
separate from the shot list's own narrative order: sequence keyframes by
which ones' bible references are actually resolved, not by where the shot
falls in the story. Where a reference is still pending, mark the keyframe
as blocked rather than guessing at it.

**Expected output:** A complete, sequenced shot list and storyboard for the
whole short, each shot marked with its keyframe(s) and their bible
reference(s), plus a preparation list ordered by what's actually ready.

### Activity 3 — Cut and defend (20 min)

**Instructions:** Find at least one shot from an earlier draft that doesn't
earn its place — because it repeats an argument another shot already makes,
or because it doesn't serve the schedule. Cut it, and write one sentence
saying why it was cut, in the vocabulary of what it repeated or failed to
add.

**Expected output:** A shortened schedule and a one-sentence justification
for the cut.

### Activity 4 — Comprehensive Review dailies (30 min)

**Instructions:** In pairs, present your complete schedule aloud, shot by
shot, without stopping to explain anything the board doesn't already show.
Your partner asks two questions about the schedule — an ordering choice, a
dependency, a keyframe choice, or the cut you made — and you answer for the
decision on the spot, the way AFI's Comprehensive Review has fellows defend
their own workbook. Swap roles and repeat for your partner's schedule.

**Expected output:** Two questions asked and answered per schedule, with any
resulting fix noted rather than made silently after the fact.

### Activity 5 — Completion check (10 min)

**Instructions:** Walk the two spec bullets together. Does the storyboard
cover every shot in the list, in sequence, start to finish? Can you point to
one cut shot and say why it didn't earn its place? Then, as an additional
check, name each shot's keyframe(s) and confirm each one's bible reference
is either resolved or explicitly marked pending.

**Expected output:** Both spec bullets verified, or a named gap with the
decision still needed to close it; each shot's keyframe(s) named with their
bible-reference status.
