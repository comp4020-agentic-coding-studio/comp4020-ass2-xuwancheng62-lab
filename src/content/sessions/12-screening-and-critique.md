## Before the workshop

Bring your near-final cut and your director's statement so far.

## In the workshop

Work in parallel groups of four or five. Each group runs its own screening
and critique round; this is not a whole-class screening. Rotate the
timekeeper and note-taker so every director receives the same time and a
written record of the feedback.

### Activity 1 — Recap and final pass (10 min)

**Instructions:** State the lecture's distinction between critique as a
specific alternative and critique as a feeling with nowhere to land. Run a
last continuity and quality check on your cut against your locked bible,
and confirm it screens unattended in under four minutes.

**Expected output:** A near-final cut, checked and timed, ready to screen
without introduction.

### Activity 2 — Screening round (40 min)

**Instructions:** Within your group, films screen back to back, no introductions and no
explanation from the director beforehand. Watch each one the way an
unattended audience would — nothing said needs to make sense of it that
isn't already on screen.

**Expected output:** Every film in the group screened cold, in sequence.

### Activity 3 — Critique and defend (40 min)

**Instructions:** After the screening round, return to each film in turn.
Divide the 40 minutes equally between directors (eight minutes each in a
group of five, ten in a group of four). Peers ask about three decisions,
proposing a specific alternative for each rather than a general impression.
The director answers for all three decisions, defending the choice
actually made against the specific alternative raised, and names honestly
where a decision was actually a limitation routed around rather than a
choice.

**Expected output:** Three directorial decisions named and defended per
director, against specific alternatives raised by their group, with notes
recorded for the revision period.

### Activity 4 — Choose the revision note (20 min)

**Instructions:** Of every note raised about your film, pick the one that
actually changes something if acted on — not the one easiest to fix, or the
one that stings most. Write it down as the one thing the revision period
will be spent on.

**Expected output:** One specific, written critique note you intend to act
on before the final cut.

### Activity 5 — Completion check (10 min)

**Instructions:** Walk the three spec bullets together. Did your cut screen
unattended, under four minutes, with no explanation needed? Did you name
and defend three decisions against specific alternatives? Do you have one
specific note you intend to act on?

**Expected output:** All three spec bullets verified.

## Afterwards

The critique you gave and received today is itself evidence for the
reflection the final project asks for. You have until the final project
deadline to revise — spend it on the note that actually changes something,
not on polish nobody named.
