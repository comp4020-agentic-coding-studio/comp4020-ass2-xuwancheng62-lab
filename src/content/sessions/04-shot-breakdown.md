## Before the workshop

Bring the scene and intent note from week 3.

## In the workshop

### Activity 1 — Recap and briefing (10 min)

**Instructions:** Recap the lecture's test in one sentence before starting:
a shot earns its place on the list only if the cut into it argues something
about whether the character is getting what they want — not because it adds
another angle on a beat you've already covered. Today's task: your week 3
scene becomes a numbered shot list, then a rough thumbnail sketch under
every entry. A shot is only finished once both its type and its one-clause
purpose are written down; the workshop ends with a peer check against the
two spec bullets above.

**Expected output:** Your week 3 scene and intent note in front of you, and
a clear one-sentence statement of today's task and how it will be checked.

### Activity 2 — Build the shot list (40 min)

**Instructions:** Working beat by beat through your scene, number each shot
and name its type (wide, medium, close-up, insert, or similar) and its
purpose in one clause. Filter every shot through the lecture's test: what
does the character want in this moment, and does this shot — cut next to
the one before it — show whether they're getting it? Don't sketch yet.
Before moving to the next beat, check your purpose against every purpose
already on the list — if two match, one of the shots isn't earning its
place; merge or cut it now, not later.

**Expected output:** A complete numbered shot list for your scene, one type
and one-clause purpose per shot, with no purpose repeated.

### Activity 3 — Sketch the thumbnails (35 min)

**Instructions:** Under each shot-list entry, sketch a rough thumbnail: a
frame, a stick figure or two, and an arrow if a body or the camera moves.
The drawing doesn't need to be good — it needs to be legible enough that
someone who has never seen your scene could stage the shot from it alone.

**Expected output:** One rough thumbnail sketch beneath every entry on your
shot list.

### Activity 4 — Dailies review (25 min)

**Instructions:** Swap shot lists with a partner — list and thumbnails only,
not the underlying scene. Working shot by shot, your partner describes out
loud what meaning they think each cut is making: what the shot shows, and
what it argues when placed next to the one before it. Wherever they can't
tell what a shot is doing from its number, purpose, and thumbnail together,
that shot is coverage, not direction — merge it into a neighbouring shot or
cut it.

**Expected output:** A shot list with every remaining shot legible to a
partner who has never read the scene, and at least one shot merged or cut as
a result of the review (or a clear note explaining why none needed to be).

### Activity 5 — Completion check (10 min)

**Instructions:** With your partner, walk both spec bullets as a checklist
against your finished list: does every shot name a type and pair with a
thumbnail, and does every shot state a purpose in one clause with no purpose
repeated anywhere on the list?

**Expected output:** Both spec bullets confirmed against your shot list by
your partner, or a named gap still to fix before the list counts as done.

## Afterwards

This list and its thumbnails are a first-draft storyboard. Week 6 expands it
into a complete sequence for the whole short, and Assignment 1 collects that
expanded version — keep this one, don't start over.
