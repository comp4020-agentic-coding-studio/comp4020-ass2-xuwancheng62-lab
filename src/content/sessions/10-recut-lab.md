## Before the workshop

Bring the continuity-checked sequence from week 9.

## In the workshop

### Activity 1 — Recap and setup (10 min)

**Instructions:** State the lecture's claim that a cut point is an
argument, not a technical seam. Identify, in your own week 9 sequence,
which shot is the strongest candidate for a reaction hold, and which
shot is the strongest candidate to repeat or reorder for a montage
effect — returning to an image the sequence already showed, or moving it
to a new position.

**Expected output:** Two named candidate decisions in your own footage —
one for a hold, one for a repeat or reorder — before cutting anything.

### Activity 2 — First cut: hold (30 min)

**Instructions:** Cut your sequence holding every reaction a beat longer
than feels natural. Don't regenerate anything — the arrangement alone is
the decision.

**Expected output:** One complete cut of the sequence, built on held
reactions.

### Activity 3 — Second cut: montage (30 min)

**Instructions:** Cut the same footage a second way, but this time change
the *arrangement*, not just a duration: repeat a shot the sequence already
used — cutting back to it a second time — or reorder a shot to a new
position, so two moments comment on each other or an image's return builds
an association the first cut didn't have. This is the lecture's "Montage
and rhythm" idea in practice, distinct from Activity 2's hold. A duration
may shift as a side effect, but the defining move has to be the repeat or
reorder itself.

**Expected output:** A second, distinct cut built on a repeated or
reordered shot, not a second duration-only variant.

### Activity 4 — Blind screening comparison (30 min)

**Instructions:** In pairs, screen both cuts for your partner without
telling them which is the hold-version and which is the montage version.
Your partner names, for each cut, what they think the scene is arguing is
its subject — before you tell them your intended sentence. Compare their
read against what you meant. Swap roles and repeat for your partner's cuts.

**Expected output:** A partner's independent read of each cut, compared
against your own intended emotional description.

### Activity 5 — Completion check (20 min)

**Instructions:** Write one sentence per cut naming the emotional
difference it produces, informed by your partner's independent read. Walk
the two spec bullets: is one cut a duration-based pacing decision and the
other a repeat or reorder, not two duration variants? Does each have its
own one-sentence description?

**Expected output:** Two one-sentence descriptions and both spec bullets
verified.

## Afterwards

Pick whichever cut actually serves your film's intent — not whichever felt
more impressive to make.
