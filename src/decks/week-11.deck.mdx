# Directing with sound

Week 11 — SLOP4555

---

## A picture-locked cut with no sound pass is still rough

No matter how finished the images look. Sound is where the last third of
the audience's attention actually lives.

---

## Direct a voice the same way you'd direct a face

A note aimed at pace, breath, or hesitation — the same discipline as week
1's line reading, now aimed at a voice instead of an expression.

*"Hold the breath half a second before the line, not after."*

---

## Ambience and Foley are a location's second bible

What a room *sounds* like is as fixed as what it looks like. A kitchen at
night has a sound signature the same way it has a fixed light and a fixed
detail — and the same amnesia problem if you don't re-supply it.

---

## Music is a directorial statement

Not wallpaper laid under a scene by default. A cue placed on purpose says
something specific about how a moment should feel — cut it, and you're
making a different claim about the same picture.

## Align a cue with the choice

Phone SFX: **6.067–24.1 s**. Music enters near the reach: **17.6 s**.
"Hey" starts **27.8 s**, after the phone reaches her ear.

The call interrupts her final packing task. Entering the music under the
reach places its claim on attempted contact rather than the first ring.

## This week's task

Direct two takes of one undirected line from your cut, using a single note
— pace, breath, hesitation. Select and lay in the take that serves the
beat. Add one ambience bed, one Foley/SFX moment, and one music cue placed
on purpose. Compare the dialogue-only and full-pass versions.
