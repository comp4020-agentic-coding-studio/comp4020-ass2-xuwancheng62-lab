# What does a director actually do?

Week 1 — SLOP4555

---

## A director doesn't make the shot

A director says what a shot is for, decides whether it did that, and
tells someone exactly what to change.

- a cinematographer
- an actor
- a model

Same loop, same craft, regardless of who's on the other end.

## What you'll be able to do after today

- tell a note apart from a wish
- turn a given objective into one playable action
- judge a take against its objective, not against "does it look good"
- tell a genuine redirect from a note that quietly changes the objective

## The chain

**intention → objective → playable action → result → interpretation**

Skip a link, and the note fails at that exact link — not randomly.

## "Before It Stops"

On the final night before her late mother's apartment is cleared, a woman
receives a call from the brother she hasn't spoken to in years — this
course's worked example, not the film you're required to make.

**This week's beat:** Alone at the kitchen table, her phone vibrates once.
Before she does anything else, she has a few seconds to decide whether to
answer.

- **Objective** — she wants to answer but consciously prevents herself
  from doing so
- **Intended interpretation** — restraint, not indifference

## A note, not a wish

| Wish | Note |
|---|---|
| "Make it more emotional" | "Look up half a second before you say it" |
| "It doesn't feel right" | "Hold the pause one beat longer before the line" |

A wish skips straight to the result. A note names the action.

---

## Redirect, or a different note?

Take A reads as boredom, not restraint. Which note fixes it?

1. "Hold the pause two seconds longer; let her jaw tighten before she
   looks up."
2. "Make her seem like she's already moved on."

Only one keeps the objective. The other directs a different character.
