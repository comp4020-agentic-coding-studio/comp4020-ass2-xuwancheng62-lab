# From story to screenplay

Week 3 — SLOP4555

---

## Form exists so you don't have to say how it feels

Screenplay form says what happens without saying how it lands. That's what
the note in the margin is for.

- sluglines, action, dialogue
- the blank space on the page matters as much as the words on it

---

## Action shows. Dialogue evades.

An action line writes what a camera can see.

Dialogue is what a character is willing to say out loud — which is usually
not what they mean.

A screenplay that writes feelings directly in either column has stopped
directing and started explaining.

---

## The margin note

*"She says this too fast, before she's decided to."*

Not a plot beat. A performance choice, aimed at one line, precise enough
that someone else could act on it without asking you what you meant.

This is the note this whole course keeps coming back to.

## "Table for One"

Drafting the closing scene: Del sliding the plate across the counter, Mara
deciding whether to eat it.

The scene on the page is one thing happening. What it's *for* is a choice
that doesn't show up in the action line at all.

---

## This week's task

Write the scene that carries your short's most weight, in proper form. Add
one intent note in the margin — how a line or action should land, not what
it is. Trade pages with a classmate: is your note specific enough that they'd
direct the same performance from it alone?

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## What you leave with

One scene, in form, with one note that survived being read by someone who
wasn't in your head when you wrote it.

It travels forward as-is: next week you break this exact scene into shots.
