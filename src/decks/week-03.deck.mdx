# From story to screenplay

Week 3 — SLOP4555

---

## Form exists so you don't have to say how it feels

Screenplay form says what happens without saying how it lands. That's what
the note in the margin is for.

- sluglines, action, dialogue
- the blank space on the page matters as much as the words on it

---

## Action shows. Dialogue evades.

An action line writes what a camera can see.

Dialogue is what a character is willing to say out loud — which is usually
not what they mean.

A screenplay that writes feelings directly in either column has stopped
directing and started explaining.

---

## The margin note

*"Her hand has already pressed to accept the call before she's registered
deciding to."*

Not a plot beat, and not "say it nervously." A concrete antecedent
circumstance, aimed at one line, precise enough that someone else could
act on it without asking you what you meant.

This is the note this whole course keeps coming back to.

---

## From premise to a scene

premise

→ decisive scene

→ scene change

→ five observable beats

→ screenplay form

→ director's note

---

## Choosing the decisive scene

Not the scene that explains the situation.

The scene where the want and the obstacle actually meet.

For "Before It Stops": the kitchen in her late mother's apartment, while
the phone rings itself out.

---

## Naming the scene change

Before writing a line of action, name two states.

**Beginning:** Nadia maintains the silence.

**End:** Nadia answers.

**Visible change:** her hand moves from restraint to accepting the call.

---

## Five observable beats

1. Theo calls.
2. Nadia sees his name but does not answer.
3. The ringing continues and the opportunity narrows.
4. Her hand moves toward the phone and stops.
5. On the final ring, she accepts the call.

Something the audience can see or hear. Not a shot, not a cut — that's
next week.

---

## One beat, converted

Beat: *On the final ring, she accepts the call.*

```
On the last ring, her hand closes over the phone. She answers.

NADIA
Hey.
```

No adjective. No stage direction about how she feels.

---

## The worksheet

Six steps, one page: story anchor, scene change, five beats, screenplay
form, director's note, final check.

Download and read it before the workshop. Fill it in during the workshop,
on your own premise.

Nothing on it is graded by itself.

## "Before It Stops"

Drafting the closing scene: on the last ring before the call would go to
voicemail, Nadia finally accepts and says "Hey," then listens.

The scene on the page is one thing happening. What it's *for* is a choice
that doesn't show up in the action line at all.

---

## This week's task

Write the scene that carries your short's most weight, in proper form. Add
one intent note in the margin — the antecedent circumstance behind a line
or action, not an adjective for how it should land. Then a classmate reads
only that one line or action plus your note — not the rest of the scene —
and describes the performance choice(s) the circumstance could motivate.
Only then do you reveal the scene and compare their reading to what you
intended.

---

## What you leave with

One scene, in form, with one note that survived being read by someone who
wasn't in your head when you wrote it.

It travels forward as-is: next week you break this exact scene into shots.
