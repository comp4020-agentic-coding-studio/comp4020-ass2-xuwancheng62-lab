# Continuity and production

Week 9 — SLOP4555

---

## Same room, same night

Shots generated separately need to look like they happened in the same
room, on the same night. This week is about noticing exactly where they
don't — and fixing only that.

---

## What has to match, and what the eye forgives

| Has to match | The eye forgives |
|---|---|
| Wardrobe, prop position | Small lighting drift between takes |
| Geography — where a door is | A slightly different lens feel |
| Screen direction | Minor skin-tone variance |

Continuity failures are specific, not a vague "it looks a bit off" — name
the thing, not the feeling.

---

## Transitions are a directorial choice

Not whatever the software defaults to. A cut, a dissolve, a hold — each
says something different about how much time passed and how connected two
moments are.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

## The repair discipline

Fix the shot that's actually broken. Not the four around it that already
work.

A continuity log names the problem, the decision — fix or accept — and the
reason. Only regenerate what the log flagged.

---

## This week's task

Cut your generated shots together in order. Watch it back once, cold, and
log every continuity break you notice. For each one, decide fix or accept,
and write why.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## What you leave with

A cut sequence, 30–60 seconds, and a continuity log with at least one real
finding and a defensible decision.

This is the raw material week 10's recut and week 11's sound pass both
build on.
