# From frame to performance

Week 7 — SLOP4555

---

## The anchor frame is a locked starting point

Generated straight from the bible — not a fresh guess, not a new
description. It matches everything already decided in Assignment 1.

Directing starts *after* the frame exists: not what's in it, but what
changes inside it over the next few seconds.

---

## Prompting for performance, not decoration

Image-to-video controls are a tool to operate, same as any camera. What you
direct with them is:

- what changes in a face, a hand, a held breath
- not what's in the room

A performance note aimed at "the lighting" or "the background" isn't a
performance note.

---

## Timing and camera movement are directable variables

Exactly like a line reading. A held beat, a delayed reaction, a slow push —
each is a choice you can name and redirect, the same way you'd redirect a
line.

---

## "Table for One"

Animating the plate-slide: the anchor frame, then two directed attempts at
the timing of Del letting go of the plate.

Same gesture, same frame — the only thing that changes between takes is
when the hand releases.

---

## This week's task

Generate an anchor image for one shot from your Assignment 1 bible.
Animate it. Write one performance note — a timing change, a held look, a
delayed gesture — and generate a second take from that note alone.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## What you leave with

An anchor image and two takes that differ by exactly one directed choice.

The same discipline as week 1 — now with motion attached.
