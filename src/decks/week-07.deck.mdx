# From frame to performance

Week 7 — SLOP4555

---

## The anchor frame is a locked starting point

Generated straight from the bible — not a fresh guess, not a new
description. It matches everything already decided in Assignment 1.

Directing starts *after* the frame exists: not what's in it, but what
changes inside it over the next few seconds.

---

## The moment before

Judith Weston's technique: invent the concrete circumstance just before the
scripted action, instead of relying on a script's bare "(pause)."

A moment-before names a fact. "She's torn" names a mood — nothing to play.

---

## A fact, not a mood

| Mood word | Moment before |
|---|---|
| "More hesitant" | "His call interrupts her final packing task" |
| Nothing to play | A specific antecedent that directs the timing |
| Judged by whether it looks dramatic | Judged by whether the antecedent is legible in the take |

## This week's task

Pick one shot from your Assignment 1 shot list. Generate its anchor image
from your locked bible. Write a moment-before for its central beat, and
keep that antecedent fixed while writing directions A and B varying one
performance choice. Generate both takes, then screen them for a partner
before revealing the directions — a dailies-review check on whether the direction
actually reads.
