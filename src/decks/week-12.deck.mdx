# Screening, critique, and the revision to final cut

Week 12 — SLOP4555

---

## The critique skill, turned on your own film

Week 9 taught you to name exactly what's broken in a sequence and decide
fix or accept. This week asks the same question about the film you made,
in front of your workshop group.

---

## Lecture models; workshop practises

Lecture: quality checks, specific critique, decision defence and honest
limits, demonstrated on "Before It Stops" excerpts.

Compare a vague defence with one naming an alternative; practise one
critique and a fix-or-retain judgement.

Workshop: groups of **4–5**, cold screenings, three defended decisions per
director, then one note to act on before the deadline.

---

## A ritual shared across real directing programs

AFI's dailies review, NYU's weekly Master Class, NFTS's rushes cinemas —
work in progress screened to a group on a fixed cadence, not just marked
at the end.

The workshop adapts that format in parallel small groups: screen cold,
then defend three decisions.

---

## Critique means naming a decision, not a feeling

*"I'd have cut to her reaction sooner"* is a critique. *"It felt slow"*
isn't — it has nowhere to land, the same problem a wish has versus a note.

Defend the choice you actually made, against the specific alternative
raised.

## A worked defence

*"Show Theo's photo somewhere."* — Cut in week 6: the reach-and-stop insert
already argues the estrangement.

*"Start music under the first ring."* — The actual Week 11 cue enters near
the reach at 17.6 s, placing its claim on attempted contact.

*"Cut the wait before the reach."* — A pause may let the packing-task
interruption register; B also missed immediate response. Distinguish an
editorial decision to retain the pause from the generated timing failure.

Three practice challenges to recorded choices, not a report of a class screening.

---

## Authorship: choice versus limitation

Separate a directorial decision you made on purpose from a limitation you
routed around. Both are real. Only one is a choice you'd defend.

## What actually transfers

**direct → generate → evaluate → redirect**

This loop doesn't belong to this semester's specific tools. It's the same
loop from week 1, and it's what's left once the tools change.

---

## This week's task

In groups of four or five, screen your near-final cut with no introduction.
Then divide 40 minutes equally between directors. Peers propose alternatives
for three decisions; defend each choice. Rotate timekeeper and note-taker.
Leave with one critique note you intend to act on.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## What you leave with

A near-final cut that's been defended out loud, and one specific note to
act on before the final deadline.

Spend the revision period on the note that actually changes something —
not on polish nobody named.
