# Building a consistent world

Week 5 — SLOP4555

---

## A model doesn't remember yesterday

A human crew remembers what a costume looked like yesterday. A model
doesn't carry anything between calls unless you hand it back — in the same
words — every single time.

That single fact is why this week exists before a single frame gets
generated.

---

## A character bible entry

Specific enough that two separate generations produce the same person:

- age, build
- wardrobe
- one distinguishing feature
- voice

Adjectives like "mysterious" don't survive a second generation. Concrete
details do.

---

## A world bible entry does the same job for place

What's on the walls, what the light looks like, what has to stay fixed shot
to shot. A location has continuity the same way a person does — and the
same amnesia problem.

---

## The hard problem, specifically for an AI cast and crew

This isn't merely good practice — it's the load-bearing discipline of the
whole second half of the course. There is no continuity unless you
re-supply it, every time, in the same words.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

## "Table for One"

Bibles for Del, Mara and the diner: what has to hold steady for the closing
scene to read as one place, not three separately generated rooms that
happen to share a name.

---

## This week's task

Bring a bible entry for every named character and location in your short.
Swap with a partner who knows nothing about your film — they describe your
protagonist back to you from the entry alone. Wherever their description
drifts, the bible was vague, not their reading.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## What you leave with

A bible that survived being read by a stranger.

This is the reference every later generation gets handed back, unchanged,
every single time — for the rest of the course.
