# Editing and dramatic rhythm

Week 10 — SLOP4555

---

## Same shots, two different stories

The same shots, cut two different ways, tell two different stories about
what a character feels. Nothing gets regenerated this week — the footage
from week 9 is the raw material, and the decisions are all in the
arrangement.

---

## A cut is a decision about what's noticed next

Not just where one shot happens to end. Every cut point is a choice about
what the audience is allowed to keep watching, and what it's asked to leave
behind.

---

## Pacing and shot duration as instruments

Holding on a reaction versus cutting away from it early are opposite
directorial choices, not a technical preference.

- hold → the audience sits inside the feeling
- cut away → the audience is denied it, on purpose

---

## Montage and rhythm

What repetition and juxtaposition do that a single long take can't:
compressing time, building an association, letting two moments comment on
each other without a line of dialogue saying so.

---

Recutting the diner sequence two ways: cutting to Mara's reaction
immediately, versus holding on Del first.

## This week's task

Take your continuity-checked sequence from week 9. Cut it twice — hold every
reaction a beat longer than feels natural, then cut away before it lands.
Write one sentence per cut on what it does to the audience's read.

---

## What you leave with

Two distinct cuts of the same footage, and a sentence per cut naming the
emotional difference.

Keep whichever actually serves your film's intent — not whichever felt more
impressive to make.
