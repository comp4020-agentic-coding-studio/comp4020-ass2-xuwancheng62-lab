# Planning before generating

Week 6 — SLOP4555

---

## Spend the hour, don't skip it

Every hour spent planning here is an hour not spent waiting on a queue for
a shot that was never going to be in the film.

This is the last week before generation starts. The discipline is to spend
it.

---

## A schedule, not a wish list

A wish list orders shots by how exciting they are to imagine.

A schedule orders them by dependency: the locked screenplay, the locked
bible, and the exact reference version each shot needs.

A shot ahead of an unresolved dependency isn't ready to generate.

---

## Bible, keyframe, generated shot

Three different jobs, not one:

- **Bible** — fixed identity: appearance, costume, props, spatial
  relationships, lighting, visual style.
- **Keyframe** — this shot's specific still: framing, pose, expression,
  prop position. Label opening input and ending evaluation target; a held
  shot can keep the same state.
- **Generated shot** — the keyframe(s) plus performance, action, timing,
  and camera movement.

## The whole short as a schedule

| # | Shot | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Clearing the kitchen, settling at the table | Kitchen + Nadia references |
| 3–5 | Wide, phone lights up, close-up still | Kitchen, Nadia, phone references |
| 6–7 | Reach and stop, then accept and "Hey" | Nadia + phone references |

The Nadia, kitchen, and phone references now exist. One dependency is
still open: the kitchen's full production approval.

---

## Worked example: shot 7's two keyframes

Label what starts the take and what you judge at its end:

- **Start/input** — low framing on her hand and the phone, still on the table.
- **End/target** — the phone at her ear as she says "Hey."

Start before the action: unsealed box → sealed box; standing → seated;
dark phone → lit phone. For shot 7, direct the lift and upward tilt.
A separate end image is optional unless the chosen workflow needs it.

---

## Matching keyframes isn't the finished shot

A take can land exactly on both keyframes and still fail as a shot.

The movement between them — performance timing, camera movement — is a
separate directorial decision. It has to be directed and evaluated on its
own terms. Week 7 develops this in depth.

---

## Tool-dependent input

Some tools accept both a starting and an ending reference image — supply
both.

Some accept only a single starting image. There, the start keyframe is the
generation's input, and the end keyframe becomes what you evaluate the
resulting take against.

Check what your tool actually supports.

---

## Narrative order vs. preparation order

Shots 1–2 need expanded room geometry and the crossing route, still awaiting
production approval. Both need Nadia; shot 2 also needs the phone reference.

Shot 6's keyframe needs only Nadia and the phone, both already resolved —
so it can be prepared first, even though it happens later in the story.

## Defending a cut: AFI's Comprehensive Review

An earlier draft included a photo of young Nadia and Theo, to explain the
estrangement.

Cut — the reach-and-stop insert already argues that. Two shots making the
same argument is one too many.

---

## Present it aloud, not just on paper

AFI's Comprehensive Review has fellows present their own workbook and
answer for it, not submit a document that's only ever read silently.

This week's workshop borrows that format directly.

---

## This week's task

Present your complete schedule aloud, shot by shot, without stopping to
explain what the board doesn't already show. Your partner asks two
questions; you answer for the decision on the spot — including the shot you
cut.
