# Reflection

Updated 2026-09-13. This account draws on the working notes and current
project records. It remains a draft for author review, not a completed crit
submission or a report of student testing.

## What moved the work forward

The breakthrough was reading the course in the order a student would use it,
rather than checking only whether the required content existed. At the
screenplay stage, I could understand the expected result but still could not
start writing. That distinction led to Week 3's worksheet: a sequence from
premise through scene change and observable beats to screenplay form. A
follow-up correction aligned its use across lecture, deck and workshop:
read it before class and fill it in during class. The support is implemented;
whether it helps beginners still needs a real trial.
[Evidence](src/content/sessions/03-screenplay-workshop.md)

Week 1 made me reconsider the cost of generation-based exercises.
Independent A/B generations introduced changes in identity, setting and
action that obscured the effect of the directing note. Instructor-provided
material reduced that variability but created a substantial preparation
burden. I ultimately retained the existing Take A and narrowed the workshop
to diagnosis, a written redirect and peer checking. This makes preparation
simpler, while giving up verification of a new performance in the workshop.
A partner can check whether a note is specific and targets the problem;
they cannot establish that it produces a better take. The lecture retains
its real A/B demonstration, while the workshop practises part of that process.
[Evidence](resources/week-01/workshop-brief.md)

## What changed about the developer I want to be

I want to build around actual use and evidence. Week 6's internal asset
identifiers were technically accurate but gave students little help in
acting on the instructions. Assignment 1's demand that all decisions be
resolved sounded complete but exceeded Week 5's taught approach to pending
fields. These revisions showed me that a successful build and valid links
answer different questions from understandable tasks and fair assessment.
The latter still require human reading and judgement.
[Evidence](handoffs/week-06.md)

I also learned to give agents explicit decision boundaries. Wording and link
repairs can proceed directly; changes to what is taught or assessed need a
proposal and my approval. More review does not automatically improve the
result, so I limited routine content checks and required reuse of findings
that remain valid. That may reduce repeated work, but local review can miss
distant dependencies and cannot guarantee whole-course coherence.
[Rules](CLAUDE.md)

## What I still need to improve

Durable records depend on timely maintenance. A numbered-activity requirement
was previously lost across context resets, prompting shared rules and short
handoffs. This time MANIFEST still said NOT STARTED even though workshop
material existed and Take A was embedded in the page. Records were not
synchronized, and the agent initially repeated the stale status. Another
file alone would not solve this: the missing step was checking and updating
it. In future, I want generation, QA and integration work to end with a
comparison between actual files and the manifest, including failures and
partial completion. That is a proposed improvement, not a demonstrated cure
for omissions.
[Audit record](MANIFEST.md)

Week 9's dark-phone entry repair and Week 10's reaction-duration comparison
also reinforced the value of preserving failures, decisions and limits.
The second trim corrected the visible entry problem, but accepted continuity
differences remained in the rough cut. Timeline checks established the
picture change, not the audience's emotional response. My next step should
include actual viewing, listening and worksheet trials, separating self-review
from outside feedback and technical verification from effects on users.
